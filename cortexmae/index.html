How to apply a foundation model to downstream tasks

16 s of fMRI · flat map

01

Freeze the foundation model. Input an fMRI time series through the pretrained encoder.

02

Read out latents. One forward pass produces 364 × 768 values for each 16s time series.

03

Train/eval a lightweight adapter. E.g., mean pool the latents and fit linear regression.

6:55-7:20. Three beats. This is how a finished foundation model becomes useful before we compare evaluations. (0) A 16-second flat-map clip passes through the pretrained CortexMAE encoder with all encoder weights frozen. The looping clip is assets/video/infer_flatmap_raised.mp4, a raised-surface-canvas version of the tight flat-map crop from assets/video/benchmark_flatmap.mp4. (1) One forward pass returns 364 patch tokens with 768 features each. That latent representation can be extracted once and reused; in the CortexMAE quickstart this is model.run_embedding and embeds.patch_embeds. (2) Labels enter only through a small task-specific probe. For the illustrated ABIDE autism example, the paper mean-pools the latent tokens and fits logistic regression. Dynamic state tasks instead use a small attentive probe over the latent sequence. Strictly speaking, this last step is supervised probe training; at test time the frozen encoder and trained probe together produce the prediction. The displayed control prediction is illustrative, not a reported subject result. This distinction sets up the next slides: fair model comparison requires matching preprocessing, splits, probe design, and tuning budget. [Sources] Lane et al., ICML 2026, Section 4 and Table 1 (364-token flat-map sequence; ABIDE autism classification; logistic regression on average-pooled embeddings over 100 randomized splits), Appendix B.4 (attentive probe protocol); CortexMAE quickstart, model.run_embedding and embeds.patch_embeds.shape (github.com/MedARC-AI/CortexMAE).

8:15-9:30. The second problem is comparability. Prior papers often use the same source dataset names, but differ in curation, preprocessing, subject splits, normalization, probe design, tuning budget, and metrics. Brainmarks reproduces the encoders behind one interface and holds the downstream experiment fixed, so the frozen encoder is the intentional variable. It supports the eight model families currently shown in this talk and evaluates eight core tasks across seven public datasets. The suite retains the commonly reported trait benchmarks and adds two relatively under-studied dynamic state benchmarks. Traits use one resting-state sample per person over 100 randomized splits. State tasks use 16-second windows and fixed held-out-subject splits with an attentive probe. That creates the apples-to-apples comparison needed to judge whether reported improvements are actually model improvements. [Sources] Lane et al., ICML 2026, Sections 1 and 4, Appendix B.4, and datasets in Table 1. Brainmarks code, model integrations, curated data, and splits: github.com/MedARC-AI/Brainmarks. Omni-fMRI was added in a post-paper Brainmarks evaluation in July 2026.

13:20-14:50. Three steps, one keypress each. (0) We scaled flat-map pretraining from 0.35 million frames, about 110 hours and 50 people, to 6.6 million frames, about 1.8 thousand hours and 880 people. Five corpus sizes, ten runs in all, one recipe; each point is the best validation loss of one run on held-out HCP-YA subjects. (1) In-distribution, the losses sit on a strict power law, L proportional to N to the minus 0.014, fitted on the three smallest corpus sizes only; the dashed part is pure extrapolation and the large runs land on it. This mirrors language modeling, though the exponent is about ten times weaker than Kaplan et al. (2) Out-of-distribution is different: NSD reconstruction improves, but from about one million frames the runs bend away from their own small-data law (exponent -0.017 on the three smallest). Greater diversity, not just greater scale, looks necessary for generalizable representations. Downstream, the COCO24 probe gain from smallest to largest is about five points (Figure 7c). [Sources] Lane et al., ICML 2026, Figures 7a and 7c, Section 5.2. Dot coordinates and fit trajectories extracted from the vector figure source (data_scaling.pdf).

Takeaways and future directions

Takeaways

Representation matters.

Flat maps provide an efficient, geometry-aware input and lead dynamic state decoding.

Evaluation can dominate rankings.

Shared normalization moves scores by 40 to 75 points. State tasks separate models more clearly than traits.

Scale is distribution-dependent.

More frames help in-distribution, but 36M UK Biobank frames do not beat 7M diverse HCP-YA frames.

Future directions

Connect structure and function.

Build a structural MRI foundation model, then align it with CortexMAE so downstream models can use anatomy and functional dynamics together.

Scale with curated diversity.

Pretrain on carefully harmonized multi-site fMRI spanning more tasks, scanners, and populations, with out-of-distribution generalization measured explicitly.

17:15-18:05. Top half: three conclusions. Flat maps are the strongest representation for dynamic state decoding. Brainmarks shows that preprocessing and evaluation choices can change the apparent model ranking. Scaling is not just frame count: the HCP-YA and UK Biobank comparison points to pretraining distribution, task diversity, scanner diversity, and preprocessing consistency as likely determinants of transfer. Bottom half: two active directions. First, build a structural MRI foundation model and connect it to CortexMAE so downstream tasks can combine anatomy with functional dynamics. Second, scale pretraining across carefully harmonized multi-site fMRI with broader coverage of tasks, scanners, and populations, and make out-of-distribution evaluation a design constraint. Careful preprocessing is intended to improve comparability, but generalization still has to be measured rather than assumed. [Sources] Lane et al., ICML 2026; github.com/MedARC-AI/CortexMAE and github.com/MedARC-AI/Brainmarks. Structural-functional integration and curated multi-site scaling are ongoing future directions supplied by Paul Scotti, July 30, 2026.

Same architecture with random init collapses (58.7 Task21, 7.0 COCO24): the pretrained video weights do the work. Trait scores are on par with all models, consistent with unreliable trait benchmarks.

Backup. Unpublished follow-up by Connor Lane, July 2026; treat as preliminary until replicated and released. Flat maps resized to 224 by 224 RGB for video-model input; standard Brainmarks probe protocol. Loading the official MAE-st checkpoint needed a non-default head dimension (facebookresearch/mae_st issue 17). [Sources] Connor Lane internal follow-up; Lane et al., ICML 2026 for the fMRI model scores.
Backup 9

Warm-starting from video reaches the same ceiling, faster.

Backup. CortexMAE pretraining initialized from the official MAE-st checkpoint versus random init, two learning rates by two seeds. The warm start converges almost immediately, near-final probe accuracy by epoch 10, but both initializations plateau at the same roughly 30 percent COCO24 level. Read both ways: video priors are a free head start, and the plateau is set by data rather than by the training recipe. [Sources] Connor Lane internal follow-up, July 2026.

Three representations, one recipe.

400 Schaefer region averages per frame.

400 patches of 4 × 1
85M params · 11 h train

77K cortical pixels on a 224 × 560 image.

364 patches of 4 × 16 × 16
86M params · 28 h train

132K cortical voxels on a 91 × 109 × 91 grid.

465 patches of 4 × 8 × 8 × 8
87M params · 50 h train

Same ViT-B encoder, same MAE-st recipe, similar patch counts. Only the input changes.

Backup. Use if asked how the three representations differ mechanically. All three train the same ViT-B with the same MAE-st recipe; only the patch embedding changes, and patch counts are similar by design (400, 364, 465), so the comparison isolates the representation rather than the token budget. Parcels: Schaefer-400 region time series, each patch is one region over 4 frames (4 by 1), 85M parameters, 11 hours on one H100. Flat map: 224 by 560 image with 77K valid pixels, patches of 4 by 16 by 16, 86M, 28 hours. Volume: 91 by 109 by 91 grid with 132K cortical voxels, patches of 4 by 8 by 8 by 8, 87M, 50 hours. Temporal patch p_t = 4 by default. [Sources] Lane et al., ICML 2026, Section 3; parameters and train times from Table 3.
